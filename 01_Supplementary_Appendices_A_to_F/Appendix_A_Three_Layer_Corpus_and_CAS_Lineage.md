## A.1 Architectural Rationale: Decoupling Geometric and Operational Information Across Three Autonomous Layers

In brownfield and legacy road, railway, port, and airport infrastructure managed by public transportation agencies—such as Colombia's *Agencia Nacional de Infraestructura* (ANI) and *Instituto Nacional de Vías* (INVIAS)—semantic interoperability fails when domain workflows attempt to embed dynamic operational, financial, and contractual state directly inside monolithic geometric Building Information Modeling (BIM) or Geographic Information System (GIS) files. Geometric representations evolve at survey or design cadence, whereas toll traffic series, pavement structural fatigue measured in equivalent single axle load (`ESAL`), unit price analysis (`APU`) schedules, and concession governance records mutate continuously at transactional cadence.

Following the architectural paradigm articulated for legacy infrastructure interoperability, SITI enforces a strict separation between **Language A** (physical-spatial and functional classification of geographic zones, modal infrastructure complexes, functional spaces, constructive systems, and physical elements across Tables `A10` through `A50`) and **Language B** (contractual management, institutional actors, resources/rolling stock, work breakdown `APU` activities, and technical/regulatory parameters across Tables `B10` through `B50`). Rather than mutating source files or duplicating geometry inside operational records, SITI distributes authority across three orthogonal, non-redundant computational substrates and reconstructs cross-domain relationships dynamically at query time:

1. **Layer 1 (`C1` — Normative Semantic Substrate: OWL 2 DL, SKOS, W3C SHACL, and ISO 16739-1:2024):** Establishes the canonical taxonomy (`16,230` active unique classes across 10 authoritative CSV tables and 7 hierarchical levels), description logic TBox axioms (`438,194` RDF triples, `157` object properties under 3 organizing superproperties, and `814` existential/universal restrictions), multilingual terminology (`2,892` reviewed SKOS expressions across 5 languages), closed-world validation shapes (`104` W3C SHACL node shapes), and standardized property/quantity definitions (`849` ISO 16739-1:2024 `Pset` and `Qto` templates).
2. **Layer 2 (`C2` — Topological Articulation Substrate: Labeled Property Graph in openCypher):** Materializes the structural and operational coupling topology as a directed acyclic taxonomic hierarchy (`16,232` deterministic `UUID` version 5 nodes and `16,228` `:SUBCLASE_DE` edges) augmented with `793` deduplicated inter-tabular and transversal edges (`A10->A50`, `A<->B`, and `Bx<->By` across `43` `openCypher` edge types) and `13` canonical assembly recipes (`REC-A-01..07` and `REC-B-01..06`). Layer 2 enforces zero intra-tabular meronymy (`0` edges where source and target share the same table) and isolates localized dependency neighborhoods via exact Markov Blankets $\partial\mathcal{M}(v)$.
3. **Layer 3 (`C3` — Columnar Analytical and Bimodal Spatial Lakehouse in Apache Parquet and DuckDB):** Stores immutable empirical observations, bimodal spatial geometries (`EPSG:9377` / `EPSG:4326` Well-Known Binary polygons/lines coupled with bimodal 64-bit EPSG:9377 anchor + 32-bit local ENU offset $4\times 4$ affine transformation matrices), contractual telemetry, toll collections, and decoupled `APU` cost structures across `2,664` active Apache Parquet datasets (`3,880,617` rows; `569,916,655` bytes).
